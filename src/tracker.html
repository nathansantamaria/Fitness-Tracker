What can you actually measure?

Answer for the tools you own and will really use, not the ones you mean to buy. Panels you cannot feed get switched off, and you can switch them back on any time.

Worth saying plainly. This tool is only as good as the numbers you feed it. Guessed portions, workouts you did not really do, and a weigh-in you skipped because you knew you would not like it all produce a dashboard that looks fine and tells you nothing. Bioimpedance body fat from a scale moves with how hydrated you are, so read it as a trend over weeks, never as a measurement. If you log roughly, the charts still work, but treat the trend lines as the truth and the daily numbers as noise.

      Strength index

      Log two sessions of the same lift and this starts moving.

      How it works: every set becomes an estimated one-rep max using a rep-to-%max slope matched to the lift type (compound, isolation, leg press), fitted to the largest reps-vs-%1RM meta-analysis (Nuzzo 2024). Pull-ups and dips count 95% of your body weight, the Smith bar and leg-press sled are added in, and sets past 15 reps are left out. Each lift scores its best two sets. A regression then separates each exercise’s own level from your overall strength on each day, so new or swapped exercises fit in without skewing the line, and one bad set (a pre-fatigued pushdown, a different cable) is trimmed rather than trusted. The line is smoothed for normal day-to-day noise (~4%), the shaded band is the 90% range, and it only calls you stronger or weaker when the change clears that noise. The dotted line adjusts for body weight, so you can see strength-per-pound rising as you cut.
